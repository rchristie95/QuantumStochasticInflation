SHORT SLIDE 004 | 00:43–01:38 | Gradients and separate regions

“On scales much larger than the Hubble radius, spatial gradients contribute little to the field’s evolution. With our cutoff \(k_{\mathrm c}=\sigma aH\), gradient terms are suppressed by \(\sigma^2\). Choosing \(\sigma\) small therefore lets us approximate each patch as spatially homogeneous. Within each patch, we retain the field and its momentum and evolve them using approximately homogeneous equations. Slow roll is a further approximation that lets us eliminate momentum when the field has settled onto an overdamped attractor. The region that becomes today’s observable universe contains many of these patches during inflation. Each follows its own local evolution, but their histories can remain correlated because they share longer-wavelength fluctuations. The circles illustrate these local regions.”

SHORT ACTION 007 | What stochastic inflation does
“We describe each patch using a coarse-grained field, Q, defined by retaining Fourier modes below a cutoff. In de Sitter, we choose a patch of fixed physical size, somewhat larger than the Hubble radius. Its comoving wavenumber cutoff therefore grows as the universe expands, continually including new modes in Q. Stochastic inflation describes how these incoming modes affect the patch’s evolution. We measure time using N, the number of e-folds of expansion.”

"We expand the quantum field in comoving Fourier modes because these coordinates follow the expansion: each mode keeps the same wavevector label and evolves independently in the free theory. Stochastic inflation describes the field coarse-grained over a patch of fixed physical size. As the universe expands, that patch corresponds to a smaller comoving region, so its comoving wavenumber cutoff must grow. The step function therefore continually includes new Fourier modes in the definition of the infrared field."

"The long field already contains modes at the start of this interval. Watch the cutoff sweep through the next modes, with each crossing contributing a signed kick below. At the end, shorter modes still lie outside the cutoff and continue oscillating. The blue dots record crossing amplitudes; this illustration isolates boundary kicks from subsequent bulk evolution."

SHORT SLIDE 008 | 04:09–04:20 | Toy model / chapter

"To understand the quantum structure behind this, let's first replace the field by a collection of oscillators."

SHORT SLIDE 009 | 04:20–04:45 | Average coordinate and total momentum

"Take independent canonical oscillators. We retain their average coordinate and their total momentum. This choice matters: averaging the momentum as well would not give a unit canonical commutator. With the total momentum, the retained pair is one properly normalized oscillator, built from many microscopic coordinates."

SHORT SLIDE 010 | 04:45–05:34 | Add one fresh oscillator

"Now one new oscillator enters. The new average weights the previous collective coordinate by n, includes the incoming coordinate, and divides by n plus one. The conjugate total momentum simply adds the incoming momentum. These are the variables we intend to retain after the update."

"There is also a complementary output. Its position is a relative coordinate, and its momentum has the corresponding canonical weighting. Keep both pairs for now. Together they contain exactly the information in the two inputs; redefining the collective mode has not itself removed any information."

SHORT SLIDE 011 | 05:34–06:00 | A canonical and unitary redefinition

"The momentum transformation is fixed by the inverse transpose of the position transformation. The complete map is canonical and has a unitary implementation. Each output pair is canonical and the two factors commute with one another. Their state can nevertheless be entangled, as the next example shows."

SHORT SLIDE 012 | 06:00–08:57 | Product inputs become entangled outputs

"Let's see that in a Gaussian example. Start with two pure squeezed inputs. This expression is their joint four-dimensional Wigner function, which initially factorizes between the two input modes."

"For this worked example n equals one. The covariance is diagonal, with opposite squeezing in the two inputs. We retain the general coordinate names so the connection with the preceding map stays visible."

"W n joint is the joint four-dimensional Wigner function. These colour maps show its exact position-position and momentum-momentum marginals, integrating over the other coordinates. Both use the same density scale. The incoming momentum is horizontal on the right, ready for the retained output momentum to become the vertical axis."

"Now express that same Wigner function in the new canonical coordinates, denoted W n prime joint. The density maps update continuously under the coordinate change. Their tilted profiles show correlations between the output modes. For these inputs the new collective and complementary factors are entangled."

"Here is the transformed joint Gaussian explicitly. The cross terms couple the collective and complementary positions, and their momenta. This is still the same joint Wigner function in the new coordinates. Both output factors remain present, and the joint state remains pure."

"Integrate that joint Gaussian over the complementary position and momentum. The result is four over five pi, times the exponential of minus eight fifths Q squared minus two fifths P squared. This is the traced Wigner function. The next animation shows how the retained axes combine to display it."

"Watch the complementary directions contract as the retained axes move together. This motion illustrates the integral written above. The final colour map plots precisely that traced Gaussian, on the same density scale as before. The complementary mode has been traced out."

"The retained covariance has entries five sixteenths and five quarters. Its purity is four fifths. The full state was pure, but the reduced state is mixed because the two outputs were entangled."

SHORT SLIDE 013 | 08:57–09:29 | Measuring vs discarding the decoupled mode

"The paper's figure shows the selective route. The incoming vacuum shell and bulk undergo the same entangling redefinition. A rank-one measurement of the decoupled mode then selects a pure conditioned bulk state. If we instead trace out that mode, we obtain the mixed reduced state. Summing or integrating the conditioned states over all outcomes recovers precisely that same reduced state."

SHORT SLIDE 014 | 09:29–10:09 | Fock and position records

"A Fock record gives the retained wavefunction by an overlap with a number state. An ideal position record selects a slice of the joint wavefunction. These are different rank-one measurements of the same output. Exact position outcomes are generalized and have a probability density."

"With a pure joint input and an ideal recorded rank-one outcome, the conditional retained state is pure. Finite resolution with unresolved information need not preserve this property."

SHORT SLIDE 015 | 10:09–10:57 | Oscillator trajectories: n=1

"Here are three descriptions of the oscillator sequence: the unconditioned reduced state, a Fock-conditioned state, and a position-conditioned state. Initially they start from the same single oscillator."

"Add the second oscillator. Each conditional column follows its own specified record. The unconditioned column averages over all possible outcomes, and can become mixed."

"Add the third oscillator. Fock conditioning can generate negative Wigner regions, while the position-conditioned Gaussian state has a different shape. All plots share fixed scales so we can compare them directly."

SHORT SLIDE 017 | 11:06–11:49 | Canonical coarse-grained field variables

"Return to the quantum field on fixed de Sitter. The sharp Fourier window defines the retained averaged field. Its conjugate is the total canonical momentum, including the effective-volume factor shown here. This volume comes from the squared smearing kernel, rather than a literal top-hat patch."

"The equal-time commutator reduces to the squared smearing-kernel norm. The effective-volume factor cancels that norm, leaving exactly i. This gives one canonical field mode whose microscopic embedding changes with the cutoff."

SHORT SLIDE 018 | 11:49–12:10 | The homogeneous patch Hamiltonian

"The sharp Fourier window fixes the effective comoving volume. Its physical counterpart is constant in de Sitter. The bulk generator is the oscillator expression below, evolving in e-fold time. This describes pre-existing modes; the boundary redefinition contributes separately."

SHORT SLIDE 019 | 12:10–14:45 | Bulk and boundary shell

"Focus on the retained collective mode and the single active collective shell input. The full infrared sector contains other degrees of freedom. The shell arrives in its vacuum, corresponding to the fresh oscillator in the toy model."

"The left factor contains the bulk density matrix; the right is the incoming vacuum shell. We are selecting the collective algebra whose evolution we want to describe, rather than retaining the entire infrared Fock space."

"Before the boundary update, the two incoming factors form a product. The vacuum assumption fixes the channel input. Correlations between the outgoing factors will be generated by evolution and redefinition, as in the oscillator example."

"First evolve the pre-existing bulk with its homogeneous generator. This internal unitary is distinct from the change in collective coordinates caused by the entering shell. Both ingredients contribute to the complete update."

"Conjugate the bulk density matrix while the incoming shell remains in the vacuum. Nothing has been traced out. Next we include the shell and redefine the retained collective and complementary outputs."

"The complete update includes bulk evolution and canonical redefinition. It generally entangles the retained and complementary outputs. Decoupled identifies a distinct canonical factor; it does not mean the outgoing states are statistically independent."

"The old collective mode and incoming shell become the enlarged collective mode and its complementary output. This is the field version of the two-output transformation. Both outputs remain until we choose a reduced or conditional description."

"Trace the outgoing complement after the entangling redefinition. Tracing the incoming vacuum beforehand would miss the generated correlations. The resulting quantum channel acts on the retained field state, just as in the toy example."

SHORT SLIDE 020 | 14:45–15:39 | The single boundary-adapted channel

"The incoming mode functions define this boundary-adapted annihilation operator. Only one active collective shell mode couples to the retained homogeneous pair. The reduced generator therefore has one non-Hermitian Lindblad channel."

"The mode-function Wronskian and canonical field commutator give the unit annihilator commutator. This normalization ties the channel to the correctly normalized incoming shell, the field counterpart of the complementary oscillator."

"The Lindblad operator is square root of three times A. Its non-Hermitian structure carries both damping and diffusion. The boundary is therefore a specific quantum channel, with coefficients fixed by the incoming shell."

SHORT SLIDE 021 | 15:39–16:20 | The short-time joint state

"Through first order in dN, retain the vacuum and one-quantum output blocks. M zero contains the Hamiltonian and normalization correction; M one is proportional to square root of dN. The blocks contain retained-mode operators, and their labels do not assume a detector."

"The output trace removes off-diagonal blocks and adds the diagonal blocks as retained-mode operators. Their two Kraus contributions combine into a trace-preserving GKLS generator. We can now state the full quantum evolution."

SHORT SLIDE 022 | 16:20–17:17 | One quantum channel

"This is the reduced master equation. The gain term and anticommutator belong to the same dissipator. It evolves the unconditional density matrix, including its mixedness, and supplies the quantum description underlying the stochastic construction."

"The effective Hamiltonian contains the bulk oscillator terms and an extra term from the changing collective embedding. That final anticommutator contribution distinguishes the complete boundary update from bulk evolution alone."

"The channel coefficients are fixed by the shell mode functions. Damping and diffusion belong to one quantum map. The result consists of a canonical collective field mode, an effective Hamiltonian, and one boundary-adapted Lindblad operator."

SHORT SLIDE 023 | 17:17–18:27 | Back to the boundary channel

"We can now make the same choice as in the toy model. Tracing the output gives the unconditional GKLS state. Keeping an efficient rank-one measurement record gives a conditional pure trajectory when the joint trajectory is pure. These are two descriptions of the same underlying bulk-boundary update."

"On the recorded branch we condition the retained state on the outcome. The measurement choice determines the unravelling. It is a way of specifying retained information, rather than an assertion that a physical detector has been installed during inflation."

"Average over the complete record ensemble and we recover the same reduced density matrix. The displayed Fock and position examples were individual possible records. Two sample trajectories alone are not that ensemble. The averaging identity is what connects the selective and non-selective descriptions."

SHORT SLIDE 024 | 18:27–20:03 | Field POVM: read one decoupled quadrature

"For the connection to a continuous Langevin record, choose a quadrature readout of the decoupled mode. The phase selects the quadrature. The signal is the conditional expectation shown here, built from the same boundary-adapted operator that appeared in the master equation."

"The observed quadrature increment separates into a conditional signal times dN and a Wiener innovation. The innovation has variance dN. The signal depends on the current conditional state, so the record and state update must be treated consistently."

"The deterministic part of the stochastic Schrodinger equation contains the effective Hamiltonian and normalization terms. I won't unpack every coefficient here. Their role is to combine with the noise update so that an efficiently monitored pure state remains normalized and pure."

"Add the innovation term to complete the pure-state equation. Averaging its projectors returns the GKLS state. With the field-adapted quadrature phase, and in the light, super-Hubble, overdamped limit, the conditional field centre gives the one-real-noise Starobinsky equation shown at the beginning. That classical equation comes with a choice of record and a controlled limit."

SHORT SLIDE 025 | 20:03–20:43 | Massive spectator drift

"The channel also describes masses outside the light-field approximation. Its unconditional mean obeys this damped oscillator equation. Mean motion identifies the drift regime, but does not establish purity or mixedness. That requires the quantum covariance."

"The eigenvalues give the threshold at three H over two. Below it the mean is overdamped; at it the roots coincide; above it the mean oscillates while decaying. Next we compare actual Wigner densities and purities."

We use physical canonical Q and P, fixed axis ranges for each mass, and one shared logarithmic density scale. The initial covariance is a pure bulk ground state and the means are zero."

"The examples are mass over H equal to one, one point five, and two. All panels begin with pure normalized states. The lower curves track purity from the same covariance matrices. Each panel's axes remain fixed and peaks are not separately normalized, so density changes are physical rather than changes in plotting scale."

This is more information than the mean drift alone provides. The channel lets us discuss the quantum-to-classical question through an explicit reduced quantum state, rather than assuming a classical probability distribution from the start."
